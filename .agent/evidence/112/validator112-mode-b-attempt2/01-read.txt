# Live Runtime Sessions

This normative protocol is **consumer-aspirational**: consumers must enforce its
session rules in their runtime. The framework publishes definitions, two closed
runtime provenance schemas and C12 record-relational validation. These checks
establish record shape and declared invariants only; they cannot prove live
ownership, generation or lease enforcement, source/heap identity or runtime
conformance. The normative SBCL reference binding is published in
[the binding document](11_sbcl_runtime_binding.md). The optional adapter capability
remains pending feature113 in phase015. Executable session controller, SBCL binding
enforcement and all thirteen live conformance proofs remain pending phase016
features115–120.

A runtime session is a long-lived process and its mutable image used for
interactive development. An agent session is an agent's conversational or
execution context under `standard/AGF.md`; ending that context does not end the
runtime process. An agent session may reattach only after establishing the
runtime's recorded identity, ownership and outstanding work.

The requirement rows below are normative. Their evidence boundaries limit what
may be claimed even when a consumer has implemented the required behavior.

## 1. Source, image and durable records

Source is the committed, replayable input to a build. An image is mutable process
state: loaded definitions, values, caches, threads and other effects. Durable
records describe what was requested and observed outside that process. These
three state classes are distinct. Saving a record or listing loaded source does
not prove that the heap matches the source; deleted definitions and partial
operations may leave invisible state behind.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Consumers MUST distinguish committed source, mutable image and durable records; session provenance MUST NOT assert source/heap identity. | A session record is provenance only and cannot become acceptance evidence. |

## 2. Ownership and leases

Exactly one principal owns a runtime session at a time. The principal may be a
human or an agent; an agent owner also has a role and agent identity. Ownership
transfer is an explicit lifecycle act, including a human/agent shared-image
handover. It changes the owner and increments `lease_epoch`; neither a context
reset nor a newly attached client silently transfers ownership.

Every request identifies its requester and expected lease epoch. The consumer
checks the requester against the current owner before executing project code.
A former owner holding an old lease cannot submit work after transfer. Transfer
must preserve or explicitly resolve outstanding operation state before the new
owner operates; it never conceals a running operation.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R2 | Consumers MUST maintain exactly one owner, increment lease_epoch on transfer, and reject foreign requesters or stale leases before execution. | A transfer record establishes claimed ownership history, not enforcement or image consistency. |

## 3. Process generations

A generation identifies one process incarnation using a counter and an
unpredictable nonce. Starting or reconstructing a process creates a new
generation. Reattaching to the same live process preserves its generation.
Every operation carries `expected_generation`; the actual generation is
recorded with its outcome. A stale request cannot execute in a replacement
process even if its session identifier remains unchanged.

Generation changes are process identity changes, not new governed repair
budgets. Attempt accounting follows `03_circuit_breaker.md` Section 3.2 across
process, agent, context, worktree, generation, rename and stage boundaries.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R3 | Consumers MUST assign a new generation to each process incarnation and reject a stale expected_generation before execution; reattach MUST preserve the live generation. | Generation provenance does not reset contract_id, step_key or attempt and does not prove loaded-source identity. |

## 4. Serialized operations

Each request has an `operation_id` and `session_id`. A session has one serialized
operation stream with at most one outstanding operation. A second submit while
the stream is busy is rejected as `session_busy`; polling and recording a wait
result do not free the stream. Ownership and generation checks precede execution.

Operation kinds describe loading source, compiling source, evaluating, running
tests and observing. Observation may invoke project code through inspection,
printing or callbacks and receives the same execution controls. Lifecycle acts
open, transfer and close sessions without evaluating project code.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R4 | Consumers MUST identify and serialize operations, reject concurrent submits while an operation is outstanding, and treat observation that invokes project code as execution. | A polling or wait receipt cannot certify completion or authorize a second operation. |

## 5. Status and wait outcomes

Terminal operation statuses are `completed`, `condition_signalled`, `cancelled`,
`terminated` and `rejected`. Nonterminal statuses are `running`,
`cancel_requested` and `unknown`. `debugger_paused` is reserved as an optional
future state and is not emitted by the first implementation. A request to cancel
moves to `cancel_requested`; `cancelled` requires confirmation that the operation
stopped. Process termination likewise requires observed termination.

A `rejected` operation is refused and recorded without executing project code.
This includes foreign, stale-generation, stale-lease, busy and review-trial requests.

A timeout records `wait_outcome: stopped_waiting`. It stops waiting only: the
operation may still run and retain the stream. An unobserved outcome is `unknown`,
not success. Infrastructure failure remains failure in the governed step and
must not be converted into a successful operation or acceptance result.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R5 | Consumers MUST separate operation status from wait_outcome; stopped_waiting MUST NOT imply cancelled or terminated, and cancellation requires observed confirmation. | Timeout proves only the wait ended; unknown and infrastructure failures cannot be represented as success. |

## 6. Condition capture and failed loads

Conditions must be captured while their diagnostic context remains available,
before unwinding destroys stack or dynamic information. Capture must bound
printing and preserve useful diagnostics without turning diagnostic rendering
into an uncontrolled second execution. A failed load or compile may have changed
the image before signalling a condition; neither operation is presumed atomic.
The image is recorded as inconsistent after a failed or partial load. Recovery
requires reconstruction from source when consistency cannot be established.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R6 | Consumers MUST capture conditions before unwinding and MUST NOT claim image consistency after a failed or partial load or compile. | Captured diagnostics describe observed failure, not rollback or an unchanged heap. |

## 7. Fresh-environment acceptance

A warm Generator session supports development feedback only. The Evaluator
re-derives acceptance in its own execution context, defined by
`02_adversarial_tdd.md` Section 2 item 4: a fresh process **and** a fresh private
build cache **and** fixtures built from committed source. Relevant dependencies,
registries, environment configuration and external resources must be hermetic so
that another worktree or development cache cannot supply hidden inputs.
Acceptance reconstructs from committed source and declared replayable commands;
it never boots a saved development image or reuses Generator session results.

The Evaluator's QA verdict owns acceptance evidence. Session transcripts may
explain development history but cannot replace independent execution. The
accepted revision, invocation, output and actual exit status are captured by
path under `04_tool_driven_state.md` Section 5.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R7 | Evaluators MUST derive acceptance from committed source in a fresh process with a fresh private build cache, committed fixtures and hermetic relevant dependencies and resources. | Warm session results, saved development images and session provenance MUST NOT count as acceptance evidence. |

## 8. Handoff and context-reset re-entry

A handoff binds the committed source manifest, replayable commands, current
owner and lease, generation, outstanding operation and relevant external-resource
references. After a context reset, the receiving agent reads durable records,
confirms its role and ownership, checks generation and outstanding operation,
and compares its workspace to the recorded source manifest before operating.
It polls or resolves outstanding work under the same stream rules. If continuity
cannot be established, it reconstructs from committed source rather than trusting
chat, a remembered process identifier or an unverified warm image.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R8 | Handoffs MUST be reconstructible from committed source and replayable commands; re-entry MUST check durable records, role, owner, lease, generation, outstanding work and workspace identity, reconstructing if continuity is unproved. | A surviving process alone does not establish safe re-entry or acceptance. |

## 9. External resources

An image may affect files, services, databases and background workers beyond its
heap. Records identify such resources, their owner, isolation boundary and
reconstruction or cleanup command. Session serialization governs the operation
stream only; it does not synchronize independent processes or external writers.
Consumers explicitly coordinate shared resources and attribute their effects.
Acceptance supplies isolated or reproducibly reset resources and declared inputs.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R9 | Consumers MUST identify external resources and ownership, declare synchronization limits and explicitly coordinate shared effects; acceptance MUST isolate or reproducibly reset relevant resources. | A serialized session does not prove external-resource isolation, synchronization or rollback. |

## 10. Review trials

Observation-only convergent review trials receive no session endpoint and no
runtime-session permission class. They do not execute project code, including
observation helpers that execute it. Runtime acceptance evidence reaches review
through the Evaluator's QA verdict under the existing evidence rules; no new
packet field or unauthenticated transcript channel is created here.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R10 | Review trials MUST be denied a session endpoint and all lifecycle, operate and accept permissions. | Trials cannot produce runtime acceptance evidence; session records do not extend the review packet trust boundary. |

## 11. Implementation lanes

The default is one implementation lane in the existing coordination model.
A runtime session does not grant parallel implementation authority. A future
`lane_authorization_ref` may refer to separately authorized lane semantics; its
presence alone authorizes nothing. Shared ownership remains serialized, and this
protocol creates no second scheduler or multi-lane dispatcher.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R11 | Consumers MUST use one implementation lane by default; additional lanes require separate authorization and defined coordination semantics. | A lane_authorization_ref is a future hook, not a parallelism grant or scheduler. |

## 12. Durable records

Session and operation records bind identity, requester, owner, lease, generation,
source manifest, outstanding operation, status, wait outcome, diagnostics and
resource references. They preserve unknowns and failures honestly. Raw command
output stays in external evidence files, referenced by path.

The Orchestrator alone owns coordination fields and circuit counters in durable
run state. Generators record their scoped development work; Evaluators own
independently captured acceptance evidence and QA verdicts. Implementation agents
preserve fields they do not own. The two schemas and executable C12 relational
checks validate session and operation records. They establish declared record
validity, never live runtime behavior or acceptance of a warm image. The normative
SBCL reference binding is delivered. The optional adapter capability remains
pending feature113 in phase015; executable controller/SBCL binding enforcement
and live conformance proofs remain pending phase016 features115–120.

| requirement-id | normative-rule | evidence-boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R12 | Roles MUST record durable provenance and evidence by path, preserve unowned fields, and leave coordination and circuit counters to the Orchestrator. | Durable provenance MUST NOT prove source/image identity or substitute for independently captured acceptance evidence. |
